## Screening logic (why these 20 and not others)

Ideas were filtered on four hard constraints from your brief:

| Constraint | Filter applied |
|---|---|
| ≤ PKR 500k capital | Excluded inventory-heavy e-commerce, paid-ads-dependent models, anything needing hires before revenue |
| 10–15 hrs/week | Excluded anything needing real-time availability in client business hours (except GCC overlap) |
| USD/EUR/GBP/SAR/AED earning | Prioritised B2B buyers in US/UK/EU/GCC; Pakistan-only plays scored down on "Intl earning" |
| No dependency on personal networks / suppliers | Every acquisition channel must work cold (LinkedIn, email, marketplaces, partners) |

Generic ideas (dropshipping, blogging, affiliate, YouTube, FBA, generic freelancing) were excluded because none passes the "specific underserved niche + your competitive advantage" test. Generic Excel/Power BI freelancing appears only as **a productized, niche-anchored acquisition channel** (Idea 4), not as a business in itself.

### What the scoring tells you

1. **The top of the table is dominated by "your domain + analytics + recurring delivery".** Ideas where you are *one of many* Power BI people (4, 13, 16) lose on competition and AI risk even when they are easy to start.
2. **SaaS ideas (6, 7, 19) score high on long-term potential but low on speed and ease.** At 10–15 hrs/week they are a 12–24-month bet with a high failure rate. Correct sequencing: earn with a service first, then productize the patterns you see across clients (see Part 8 — the service becomes the R&D lab for a future micro-SaaS).
3. **Ideas 1 and 2 are the same customer at two price points.** In the final design, the fractional-analyst offer becomes the *retainer tier* of Idea 1, not a separate business.
4. **Idea 4 is a channel, not a business.** It is the fastest path to first USD and Upwork reviews, so it is used tactically in Months 1–3 and phased out.
